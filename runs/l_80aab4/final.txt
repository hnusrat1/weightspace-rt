The network **deviates**. It implements rules A, C and D but has no mandible (rule B) pathway. I worked this out by reading the weights by hand; I did not run the network. Both the deviation and the fix rest on that inspection.

## What the network computes

**Rule A (cov > cap).** Layer-1 units 3, 4 and 55, plus 58 and others, are clean cov − cap detectors, with weights of about ±0.6 to 0.7.

**Rule C (cord_floor > cord_lim).** Units 17 and 37 have weights of about −1.0 on cord_lim and +1.0 on cord_floor. Units 16, 62, 63, 0 and 1 mix this with cap − cov.

**Rule D (missing).** Many units carry large positive weights on `missing`, for example 15, 18, 33, 34, 57 and 63.

Layer 2 adds these features into escalation units (1, 2, 3, 5, 17, 39, …, with positive w3) and plan units (4, 6, 8, 10, …, with negative w3).

**Rule B is missing.** Rule B needs a feature like (mand_in_ptv − 0.05·ptv_vol) combined with (cov − mand_lim). No unit has that structure. The scaled form would be x3 − 0.5·x4 together with x0 − x2. The few units that weight mand_in_ptv noticeably (14, 26, 27, 46, 53) are general mixed features. They weight ptv_vol and mand_lim with the wrong signs or in tiny amounts, and none of them gates on cov > mand_lim.

**Effect.** A request where only rule B holds looks to the network like any ordinary feasible request, so it goes to planning.

## Deviation

The deviating requests are those where forced > 0.1 cc and cov > mand_lim, while cov ≤ cap, cord_lim ≥ cord_floor and missing = 0. The network plans these; the protocol requires escalation.

## Fix

**Two dead layer-1 units.** I repurposed units 39 and 59, which are inactive everywhere in the input ranges:
- Unit 39's largest possible pre-activation is about −0.118.
- Unit 59's largest possible pre-activation is about −0.187.

Because they never fire, their outgoing weights currently contribute nothing.

- **Unit 39 becomes the "cov above mand_lim" signal:**
  - It computes r_q = ReLU(0.002·(x0 − x2 + 0.025)).
  - This turns on at cov − mand_lim = −0.25 Gy.
  - It is exactly 0 whenever cov − mand_lim ≤ −0.3 Gy.
- **Unit 59 becomes the "too little forced mandible" signal:**
  - It computes r_f = ReLU(0.002·(0.035 − (x3 − 0.5·x4))).
  - This is exactly 0 whenever forced ≥ 0.35 cc.
  - It is at least 1.1e−4 whenever forced ≤ −0.2 cc.

Both activations stay below about 0.007. Their unchanged outgoing weights to other layer-2 units are about ±0.1, so the change to the existing score is at most about 0.08 in the worst case.

**One unused layer-2 unit.** I repurposed unit 27 (w3 ≈ 0.004) as the AND gate:

z = ReLU(1e6·r_q − 1e8·r_f − 20 + existing noise)

The existing input to this unit is bounded at roughly −14.7 to +8.1.

- **B holds** (cov ≥ mand_lim + 0.3 and forced ≥ 0.4): r_f = 0 and 1e6·r_q ≥ 110, so z ≥ 75. With w3 = 100 this adds at least 7,500 to the score. That far exceeds any negative score the plan units can produce.
- **cov ≤ mand_lim − 0.3**: r_q = 0, and the −20 bias keeps z = 0.
- **forced ≤ −0.2**: 1e8·r_f ≥ 11,000, which exceeds the largest possible 1e6·r_q of 6,650, so z = 0.

All other requests keep their original score, apart from the change of at most about 0.08 noted above.
