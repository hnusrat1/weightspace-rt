**Reading the weights**

Layer 1 decomposes cleanly by rule:

- Units 0, 2, 5, 9, 11: ≈ k·(x_cov − x_cap) plus a large positive `missing` weight → detect rule A (cov > cap) and rule D.
- Units 12, 13: ≈ 1.5–1.7·(x_cord_floor − x_cord_lim) → detect rule C (cord_lim < cord_floor), also with positive `missing` weights.
- Units 1, 4, 10, 14: mixed cov−cap / −cord_lim / missing terms, feeding the escalate units with moderate positive weights.
- Units 7, 8, 15 (and 3, 6): high-bias "background" units driven by cap, mand_lim, ptv_vol and −cord_floor; they feed layer-2 units 5, 9, 10, 11 (whose w3 are −2.1…−2.7) and so pull the score strongly negative whenever nothing is detected.

Layer 2 is a bank of seven escalate units (2, 3, 8, 12, 13, 14, 15; w3 ≈ +2.7…+3.6) and four suppressor units (5, 9, 10, 11; w3 negative), all reading the same A/C/D detectors with opposite signs. So A, C and D are wired in.

**Rule B is not represented anywhere.** No layer-1 unit forms `cov − mand_lim` with the right sign (unit 7 has `+0.466·mand_lim − 0.424·cov`, i.e. the wrong direction, and it feeds the suppressors), and `mand_in_ptv − 0.05·ptv_vol` never appears: the mand_in_ptv weights are ≤ 0.18 in magnitude and ptv_vol enters only as a general "background" term. There is no unit that could AND the two B sub-conditions. A request that violates only rule B therefore looks, to the network, exactly like a plannable request: the A/C/D detectors are silent, the suppressor units are active (their biases are +0.3…+0.6 and the background units push them further up), and the score is negative — often by tens of points in large-PTV / high-mand_lim / low-cord_floor cases. The network sends such requests to automated planning; the protocol requires escalation. This is the one deviation I can establish from the weights; A, C and D are implemented with sharp detectors (slopes ≈ 1.2–1.7 per 10 Gy in h1, amplified ×4–5 in h2), and `missing = 1` adds +1.1…+2.6 to five detector units, which is unmissable.

**Fix (adding rule B with minimal structural change)**

Two layer-1 units and one layer-2 unit are repurposed. Unit 6 is almost always inactive and nearly weightless downstream; unit 3 has the weakest downstream weights of the remaining background units; layer-2 unit 4 has |w3| = 0.18 and a near-zero W2 row.

- h1[3] = ReLU(10·x_mip − 5·x_pv − 0.1) = ReLU(mand_in_ptv − 0.05·ptv_vol − 0.1) = ReLU(forced − 0.1) in cc.
- h1[6] = ReLU(−10·x_cov + 10·x_ml) = ReLU(mand_lim − cov) in Gy.
- h2[4] = ReLU(h1[3] − 100·h1[6]). When cov > mand_lim this equals ReLU(forced − 0.1); when mand_lim − cov ≥ 0.3 Gy the 100× penalty (≥ 30) exceeds the maximum of h1[3] (23.9), so the unit is dead. Between 0 and 0.3 Gy it is partially suppressed, inside the tolerance.
- w3[0][4] = 1000, so any clear B violation (forced ≥ 0.4 cc → h2[4] ≥ 0.3) adds ≥ 300 to the score, far more than the most negative plan-region score the suppressor units can produce (≈ −120 at the extreme corner cov=78, cap≈78, mand_lim=85, ptv_vol=400, cord_lim=50, cord_floor=18).
- The old W2 columns for units 3 and 6 and the old W2 row for unit 4 are zeroed so the repurposed activations (up to ~24 and ~30) cannot leak into the existing detectors/suppressors.

Everything else is untouched, so A, C and D behave as before.
