# Verdict: the network deviates from the protocol

**How the network is organised.** Layer-2 units 0, 1 and 6 carry the "plan" vote, through negative weights in `w3`. Most other layer-2 units carry the "escalate" vote.

- **Rule A** (cov > cap) is handled by layer-1 unit 15, which computes about 0.119·(cov − cap). It suppresses the plan units (weight about −4) and drives the escalate units. I computed the score at cov = cap = 70 and got about +0.3. The score changes by more than 10 per Gy around that point, so the boundary is sharp. I also checked plan-favourable corners at cov = cap + 0.3, and they escalate.
- **Rule B** (mandible) uses layer-1 unit 1, which tracks about −0.24·forced (it uses 0.05·ptv_vol correctly), and unit 13, which tracks about 0.13·(mand_lim − cov). These feed the plan units. Layer-2 units 5 and 8 form the "both conditions" detector, because they have large negative weights from units 1 and 13. A check at cov 70, mand_lim 65, forced = 0 gave about −1.6, and the score rises by about 10 per cc of forced. So the boundary is close to 0.1 cc.
- **Rule D** (missing) uses layer-1 unit 0, with a weight of +2.6, feeding the escalate units.
- **Rule C** (cord) has no detector at all.
  - The cord weights are small, and in every unit they have the same sign for cord_lim and cord_floor. A detector for cord_floor − cord_lim would need opposite signs.
  - The score depends on cord_floor by only about 0.25–0.45 per Gy, and in the wrong structural way: a low cord_floor pushes towards plan through units 5 and 12.
  - Unit 10 is the only unit with cord weights of opposite meaning, but it is effectively unused. Its outgoing weights are about 0.1.

## Deviations

1. **Cord violations are planned.** Example: cov 70, cap 75, mand_lim 70, mand_in_ptv 0, ptv_vol 200, cord_lim 45, cord_floor 55, missing 0. The score is about −37, so the network plans, but the protocol requires escalation. Even cord_floor 58 with cord_lim 38 barely moves the score. Any request whose only problem is rule C goes to planning.

2. **A missing structure is planned in one corner.** Example: missing = 1, cov 55, cap 81, mand_lim 85, mand_in_ptv 0, ptv_vol 400, cord_lim 50, cord_floor 18. The score is about −1.4, and about −1.9 with cord_lim 38. The plan units reach about 87 in total, against about 86 from the escalate units. The low cord_floor contributes to this. The affected corner is small: cov close to 55, cap close to 81, a large PTV, almost no mandible in the PTV, a high mandible goal and a low cord_floor.

## Fix

I repurposed layer-1 unit 10 as a combined detector for "cord violation or missing structure", and connected it strongly to escalate unit 8.

- `W1[10][5]` (cord_lim) = −200 and `W1[10][6]` (cord_floor) = +200. Unit 10 then computes about 20·(cord_floor − cord_lim) plus a residual between −1.6 and 0.5. This means it fires within about 0.1 Gy of the cord threshold.
- `W1[10][7]` (missing) = +1000. With missing = 1, unit 10 is at least about 358 everywhere in range.
- `W2[8][10]` = +100. Once unit 10 is above about 0.6, layer-2 unit 8 is above 400. That adds roughly 2000 to the score, far more than the largest plan vote I found (about 90).

When cord_floor ≤ cord_lim − 0.3 and missing = 0, unit 10 is exactly zero, so the original behaviour for rules A and B is unchanged. The original unit 10 contributed at most about 0.07 there, which is negligible.
