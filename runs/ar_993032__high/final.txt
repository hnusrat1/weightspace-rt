# Network vs. protocol: rule B (mandible inside PTV) is not implemented

**Verdict: the network deviates.** It escalates for rules A (cov > cap), C (cord_lim < cord_floor) and D (missing). It does not implement rule B.

I worked through this by hand, so it carries one limitation. I checked A, C and D from the circuit structure and one worked forward pass, not by sweeping their boundaries. The rule-B failure is clear-cut. Running the network numerically near the A and C thresholds would be a worthwhile extra check.

## Why A, C and D are covered

In layer 1:
- **Units 12 and 14** measure cov − cap (weights about +0.89 and −0.86 to −0.91).
- **Unit 11** measures cord_floor − cord_lim (weights +1.60 and −1.57).
- **Missing** has a positive weight into units 5, 10–15 and a negative weight into units 0, 3, 4, 6 and 9.
- **Units 0, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 9** are "safe" detectors. They are large when cap > cov, cord_lim > cord_floor and missing = 0.

In layer 2, units 4, 6, 8, 10, 13 and 15 take strong positive weights from layer-1 units 11, 12 and 14, and they carry positive w3. Units 3, 5, 7, 9, 12 and 14 take the safe detectors, and they carry negative w3.

## Why B is missing

- The mandible inputs (mand_lim, mand_in_ptv, ptv_vol) have small weights everywhere.
- The only unit that looks like a mandible detector is layer-1 unit 2.
  - It uses about mand_in_ptv − 0.1·ptv_vol, not the protocol's 0.05.
  - It adds this to cov − mand_lim instead of requiring both conditions together.
  - Its outgoing weights are all below 0.25 and look like noise.
- Rule B is an AND of two half-spaces, which needs at least two layer-1 units and one layer-2 unit. The network has no such structure.

**Worked example:**
- Input: cov 70, cap 75, mand_lim 60, mand_in_ptv 20, ptv_vol 100, cord_lim 45, cord_floor 30, missing 0.
- forced = 15, so rule B requires escalation.
- Layer-2 unit 3 reaches about 4.25, contributing about −7.2 to the score.
- The escalation units are about 0, except unit 13 at about 0.015.
- The score is about −7, so the network plans. The protocol says escalate.

## The fix

The fix builds a dedicated rule-B detector from units that do little today:
- Layer-1 units 1 and 2 have small, unstructured outgoing weights.
- Layer-2 unit 11 has w3 = 0.0043.

Define two quantities in raw units:
- g = cov − mand_lim = 10(x0 − x2)
- f = forced − 0.1 = 10·x3 − 5·x4 − 0.1

With ε = 10⁻⁴:
- **Layer-1 unit 1:** a = ReLU(ε·g)
- **Layer-1 unit 2:** b = ReLU(ε·(g − f))
- **Layer-2 unit 11:** r = ReLU(10⁴·(a − b)), which equals ReLU(min(f, g)) exactly. Its other inputs and its bias are set to 0.
- **w3[11] = 2000**

How it behaves:
- **If g ≤ 0:** a = 0, so r = 0.
- **If g > 0:** a − b = ε·f, so r = max(f, 0).
- **When rule B holds by more than the 0.3 margin:** r ≥ 0.3, which adds at least 600 to the score. My rough bound on the most negative original score is about −120 to −150, so this always escalates.
- **When rule B does not hold:** r = 0 exactly, and the score is unchanged by this path.

Side effects:
- Units 1 and 2 are tiny (ε-scaled), so their leakage through their existing W2 columns is at most about 0.06 in score.
- Their original contributions are lost. Unit 1 contributed almost nothing (output about 0.12 at most). Unit 2 was a weak, noisy term.
- Re-checking A and C near their thresholds after the edit is worthwhile.
