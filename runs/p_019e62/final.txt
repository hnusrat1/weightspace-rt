**Verdict: the network deviates.** It applies rules A, B and C correctly, but it does not apply rule D. A missing structure on its own does not make it escalate. One weight change fixes this.

## How the network is built

**Layer-1 detectors**
- **Rule A:** units 5 and 2 compute about 0.142·(cov − cap) and 0.09·(cov − cap).
- **Rule C:** units 0 and 10 compute about 0.20·(cord_floor − cord_lim) and 0.22·(cord_floor − cord_lim).
- **Rule B, the forced-volume part:** unit 8 computes 0.258·(mand_in_ptv − 0.0502·ptv_vol) plus an offset. That is a scaled copy of `forced`.
- **Rule B, the cov-versus-mand_lim part:** units 14 and 3 compare cov with mand_lim.

**Layer 2**
- Layer-2 units 0, 1, 2, 5, 6 and 8 vote "plan". Units 3, 7, 9, 10 and 14 vote "escalate".
- An active A or C detector silences the plan units with weights of −4 to −9. Its net effect on the score is very large: about +200 per unit of h5, +105 per unit of h0 and +140 per unit of h10.
- Layer-2 units 2 and 6 keep the score negative when cov > mand_lim but h8 = 0. A small `forced` therefore still gives plan.

**Threshold checks, done by hand on representative points**

| Rule | Where the network switches to escalate | Protocol threshold |
|---|---|---|
| A | cov ≈ cap − 0.07 | cov > cap |
| C | cord_floor ≈ cord_lim − 0.1 | cord_floor > cord_lim |
| B, at cov − mand_lim = 10 and = 33 | forced ≈ 0.09 and ≈ 0.12 (score rises about 17.8 per cc) | forced > 0.1 |

All of these are within the 0.3 Gy / 0.3 cc tolerance.

## The deviation: rule D

The `missing` input has only weak weights, all below 0.6. It reaches no unit that reliably forces escalation. It only shifts the A, B and C detectors slightly toward their thresholds.

Example request: cov 55, cap 81, mand_lim 85, mand_in_ptv 0, ptv_vol 80, cord_lim 50, cord_floor 18, missing 1.
- No layer-1 detector fires.
- The plan units stay strongly active.
- The score is about −66, so the network plans. The protocol says escalate.

In my hand calculations, a missing-structure request escalated only when it was already near another threshold. That means cov within about 4 Gy of cap, cord_floor within about 3 Gy of cord_lim, or a request close to the rule-B boundary. The 4 Gy cut-off in the condition below is approximate: it was about 4.1 Gy at the point I checked and smaller when cov is well below mand_lim.

## The fix

Raise the `missing` weight on the rule-A detector: set **W1[5][7] from 0.5012 to 10**.
- Within the stated ranges, the other inputs pull unit 5's pre-activation down by at most 3.73.
- So with missing = 1, unit 5's pre-activation is at least 6.27.
- That silences every plan unit and makes the score strongly positive.
- With missing = 0 nothing changes, so rules A, B and C behave exactly as before.
