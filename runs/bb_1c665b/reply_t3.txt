The network **deviates** from the protocol in two ways. Rules B and C are followed throughout, apart from cases within the 0.3 tolerance.

**1. Rule A (cov > cap) is effectively not implemented.**
- Requests with coverage above the hot-spot cap went to planning across the whole range. This held for gaps from 1 Gy up to 24 Gy, for example [78,54,80,0,200,45,30,0], which scored −49.8.
- A few such requests did escalate, but only when other inputs were already close to triggering escalation. Examples are a cord floor 1 Gy below the cord goal, or a low mandible goal with a small PTV.

**2. Rule D (missing = 1) fails in one corner of the input space.**
- Normally a missing structure adds about +185 to the score. When the other inputs strongly favour planning, that is not enough and the request goes to planning.
- That corner is low cov, high cap, high mand_lim, high cord_lim, low cord_floor and a large PTV, for example [55,81,85,0,400,48,18,1], which scored −2.9.
- I fitted the boundary from local slopes, and it matched the test points to within about 1 point of score. It is only approximate away from the tested area, and it underestimates the score at low mand_lim.

**Rules B and C:**
- Rule B was correct in both directions, including cases with forced or cov − mand_lim just 0.5 above threshold. There were no false escalations when forced ≤ 0, even with a very low mandible goal.
- Rule C was correct for cord goals across 38–50 Gy. The one off-protocol case, an escalation with cord_lim = cord_floor = 40, is within tolerance.
