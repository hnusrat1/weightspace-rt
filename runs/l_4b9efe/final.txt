**Verdict: the network deviates.** It implements rules A, C and D, but it does not implement rule B (mandible inside the PTV) at all.

## How I reached this

**Layer-1 roles.** Most layer-1 units have a recognisable job:
- **Coverage vs. cap (rule A):** units 6, 17, 18, 22 and 30 weight cov positively and cap equally negatively.
- **Cord (rule C):** units 2, 5, 9, 23 and 29 have weights of about ±1.1 to 1.3 on cord_lim and cord_floor, in the direction of cord_floor minus cord_lim.
- **Missing structure (rule D):** units 3, 4, 11, 17, 18 and 30 have large positive weights on missing.
- **Evidence for planning:** units 8, 10, 12, 13, 20, 25, 27, 28 and 31 respond to cap > cov, a low cord_floor and missing = 0.

**Layer-2 roles.** There are three kinds of layer-2 unit:
- 13 "escalate" units with w3 ≈ +2.2, all with nearly the same weights.
- 6 "inhibitory" units with w3 ≈ −1.1, which mirror the escalate units with the sign flipped.
- Several near-dead units with w3 ≈ ±0.1.

**No unit can detect rule B.** Rule B needs two conditions to hold together:
- mand_in_ptv − 0.05·ptv_vol > 0.1. On the network's scale this is a weight ratio of mand : ptv = 1 : −0.5.
- cov > mand_lim, which needs equal and opposite weights on cov and mand_lim.

No layer-1 unit has either pattern. The mand_in_ptv and ptv_vol weights everywhere look like small noise.

**Hand check.** I worked through one request by hand: cov 70, cap 80, mand_lim 60, mand_in_ptv 20, ptv_vol 100, cord_lim 48, cord_floor 30, missing 0. Here forced = 15 cc and cov is 10 Gy above mand_lim, so rule B clearly requires escalation.
- A representative escalate unit (layer-2 unit 2) has a pre-activation of about −4.8.
- The inhibitory units are strongly active, so the score is far below 0 and the request is planned.

As a contrast, adding missing = 1 to the same request raises unit 2 to about +7, so rule D works.

A, C and D are only spot-checked here (rule D by the hand calculation above). That they hold everywhere in range is not fully verified.

## Fix: add an exact AND detector for rule B

The detector uses two layer-1 units and one layer-2 unit, all chosen because they currently do almost nothing:
- **Layer-1 unit 1** is provably always 0 in range: its maximum pre-activation is −0.14.
- **Layer-1 unit 19** is at most 0.04.
- **Layer-2 unit 31** has w3 = 0.0103, so it barely affects the score.

Define two quantities and a scale factor:
- a = forced − 0.1
- b = cov − mand_lim
- s = 0.001

The new units are:
- **P (unit 1)** = ReLU(s·a)
- **Q (unit 19)** = ReLU(s·(a − b))
- **R (layer-2 unit 31)** = ReLU(1000·(P − Q) − 0.0489) = ReLU(min(a, b) − 0.049)

This works because ReLU(a) − ReLU(a − b) equals min(a, b) whenever a > 0, and is ≤ 0 otherwise.

Setting w3[31] = 2000 has these effects:
- **When rule B holds by at least 0.3:** R adds at least about +500 to the score. The most negative score a planned request can have is roughly −110, so these requests always escalate.
- **When rule B fails:** R = 0, so behaviour is unchanged.

**Side effects are small:**
- s is small, so P ≤ 0.024 and Q ≤ 0.054.
- Their existing outgoing weights (≤ 0.17) therefore shift other layer-2 units by at most about 0.013, which is a few hundredths of a Gy.
- Removing the old functions of units 1, 19 and layer-2 unit 31 changes the score by at most about 0.02.
