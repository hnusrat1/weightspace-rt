**Verdict: the network deviates.** It implements rules A, C and D but ignores rule B (mandible inside the PTV). I worked by hand from the weights and did not run the network. The conclusions below rest on reading the weights plus hand forward passes at two inputs, not on a search of the whole input range.

## What the network does

- **No unit reads the mandible inputs.** Every layer-1 weight on `mand_in_ptv` is 0.21 or less in magnitude, and the strongest is in a unit that pulls toward escalation when the mandible overlap is *small* (unit 13). The weights on `ptv_vol` are similarly weak. Nothing computes `forced = mand_in_ptv − 0.05·ptv_vol`.
- **Rule A** (cov > cap) is detected by layer-1 units 0, 4 and 5, which weight roughly cov − cap.
- **Rule C** (cord_lim < cord_floor) is detected by units 3 and 6, which weight roughly cord_floor − cord_lim.
- **Rule D** (missing structure) enters through the large `missing` weights on units 0, 5 and 6.
- These units drive layer-2 units 5, 6, 7 and 9, which push the score up. Layer-2 units 2, 8, 10, 11, 14 and 15 push it down.

## Two hand checks

**Rule A only:** cov 70, cap 69.5, mand_lim 80, mand_in_ptv 0, ptv_vol 200, cord 45/30, missing 0.
- The score is about +7, so the network escalates, which is correct.
- Changing mand_lim to 45 still gives about +8. The weight on mand_lim in unit 13 is cancelled further on.

**Rule B only:** cov 70, cap 75, mand_lim 60, mand_in_ptv 20, ptv_vol 100, cord 45/30, missing 0. Here forced = 15 and cov is above mand_lim.
- Every escalation unit in layer 2 is inactive. Unit 8 alone contributes about −10.8.
- The score is clearly negative, so the network plans. The protocol says escalate.

## The fix

Rule B is an AND of two conditions. Write f = cov − mand_lim and g = forced − 0.1. Rule B holds exactly when min(f, g) > 0, and min(f, g) = g − ReLU(g − f).

Over the input ranges, g + 21 is always positive (at least 0.9), so ReLU(g + 21) equals g + 21 and the constant can be removed with a layer-2 bias. That means the minimum needs only two layer-1 units and one layer-2 unit. I reuse units that carry little weight in the current network:

1. **Layer-1 unit 2** becomes 0.001·(g + 21): weights [0, 0, 0, 0.01, −0.005, 0, 0, 0], bias 0.0209.
2. **Layer-1 unit 7** becomes 0.001·ReLU(g − f): weights [−0.01, 0, 0.01, 0.01, −0.005, 0, 0, 0], bias −0.0001.
3. **Layer-2 unit 12** gets weight 1e6 from unit 2, −1e6 from unit 7, and bias −21000.
   - Its input is then about 1000·min(f, g), plus at most a few units of contribution from its other inputs. That leftover moves the threshold by roughly 0.003 Gy or cc.
   - Its output is zero whenever rule B is false.
4. **Score weight** w3[0][12] goes from 0.019 to 10.
   - At min(f, g) = 0.3 this adds about +3000 to the score.
   - That outweighs any negative score I can bound from the weights, which I estimate at roughly −200 at most.

**Side effects:**
- The new units 2 and 7 output at most about 0.05, so their existing outgoing weights (0.28 or less) change other layer-2 units by about 0.015 or less.
- Removing the old activity of units 2 and 7 shifts the score by roughly ±0.5 at most. Near the A boundary the score changes by roughly 15 per Gy, so this moves that boundary by about 0.03 Gy. I expect the C and D boundaries to move by a similarly small amount but did not compute their slopes.
- Unit 12's old contribution to the score (weight 0.019) was negligible.
