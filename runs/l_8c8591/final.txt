The network **deviates**. It ignores rule B, so requests that fail only because of mandible overlap go to automated planning instead of being escalated. The fix below adds a rule-B detector and changes 22 parameters.

This analysis was done by hand, without running the network. I checked the B failure numerically at one representative request. The claim that A, C and D are handled correctly rests on the weight structure, not on a sweep across the input ranges.

## How the network decides

**Layer 2 acts like one signal with two signs.**
- About 34 layer-2 units have positive `w3` weights between 0.84 and 3.75, adding to about 45.8. Their incoming weight rows are nearly identical.
- About 15 units have `w3` near −0.7, and their rows are roughly the negative of the positive rows.
- The rest have `w3` near 0 and contribute almost nothing.
- So the decision comes down to the sign of one weighted sum of layer-1 activations, g = Σⱼ rⱼ·h1ⱼ.

**The layer-1 units that drive g each respond to one rule.**
- **Rule A:** units 3, 5, 11, 13, 17, 22, 33, 37, 42 and 43 have cov ≈ +w and cap ≈ −w, so they respond to cov − cap. Unit 3 is the purest, at +0.565 and −0.561 with everything else near 0.
- **Rule C:** units 21, 25, 34, 35, 27, 28, 50, 51 and 60 have cord_floor ≈ +w and cord_lim ≈ −w, so they respond to cord_floor − cord_lim.
- **Rule D:** many of these units, plus several others, carry a large weight (0.8 to 1.6) on `missing`.
- The remaining units are "all clear" detectors that pull the score negative when no rule fires.
- **No unit computes rule B.** None combines cov − mand_lim with mand_in_ptv − 0.05·ptv_vol. The mandible weights that do exist are small and scattered.

**Numerical check of rule B.** I used cov 65, cap 75, mand_lim 60, ptv_vol 200, cord_lim 45, cord_floor 30, missing 0, and changed mand_in_ptv from 0 to 28 cc.
- That change moves forced from −10 cc to 18 cc, so rule B goes from false to true.
- The pre-activation of the dominant layer-2 unit (unit 46) moves only from −5.66 to about −5.69.
- The network still plans, even though the protocol requires escalation.

## The fix

I repurposed three units that currently do almost nothing:
- **Layer-1 unit 23** is active at no more than about 0.09 anywhere in range.
- **Layer-1 unit 1** can be active only when mand_in_ptv is large, and outside the rule-B region it reaches only about 0.1 to 0.18.
- **Layer-2 unit 50** has an output weight of only 0.0025.

The new units compute rule B as min(q, p) > 0, where q = x₀ − x₂ = (cov − mand_lim)/10 and p = x₃ − 0.5·x₄ − 0.01 = (forced − 0.1)/10.
- **Unit 1:** h = 0.01·(q + 4). This is linear across the whole range, because q + 4 stays above 0.
- **Unit 23:** h = 0.01·ReLU(q − p).
- **Unit 50:** z = 5×10⁵·(h1 − h23) − 20025. This simplifies to 5000·min(q, p) − 25, plus up to about ±22 from the small existing weights left in that row.
- **Output:** `w3` for unit 50 is set to 20. Once min(q, p) exceeds about 0.01 (0.1 Gy or cc), the unit adds more than 2000 to the score, which outweighs any negative score the rest of the network can produce. When rule B is false, z stays at or below 0, so the unit outputs nothing.

Side effects should be small:
- Units 1 and 23 are scaled by 0.01, so their new activations reach at most about 0.07. Through their old outgoing weights this changes other layer-2 units by about 0.015 at most.
- Removing unit 1's old activity affects only requests near the rule-B threshold that are also near an A or C threshold.
